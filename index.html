About Sentinel-1 SAR

How radar sees crops

Sentinel-1 is a European Space Agency (Copernicus) mission carrying a C-band Synthetic Aperture Radar. Unlike optical satellites, it sends its own microwave pulses and records what scatters back, so it works day and night and sees through clouds, which is vital during the cloudy Indian monsoon (Kharif) season.

📡

Backscatter (VV / VH)

Strength of the returned signal. Rough, vegetated surfaces scatter more energy back to the sensor.

💧

Flooded paddy

Standing water acts like a mirror (specular reflection), giving very low backscatter early in the season.

🌾

Crop growth

As rice grows, canopy structure increases backscatter, creating a distinctive temporal signature.

🕒

Temporal change

Comparing images across the season separates active crops from fallow or bare land and built-up areas.

Study Area

Where the analysis was carried out

The study covers an agricultural region in India with a total mapped extent of about 2.48 lakh hectares (≈ 2,477 km²), dominated by Kharif paddy cultivation in the Karnal district of Haryana,, India. Classification uses Sentinel-1 time-series for the Kharif season, processed in Google Earth Engine.
